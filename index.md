## About Me

Hi! I'm Mo, a junior at UNC Charlotte double majoring in Political Science (Honors) and Data Science. I grew up in Raleigh, North Carolina, and came to Charlotte to pursue a degree path that lets me combine two things I care about: the systems that shape people's lives, and the data that can reveal how well — or how poorly — those systems are working.

My interest in this intersection didn't start in a classroom. Internships at the Wake County Legal Support Center and The New North Carolina Project gave me an early, hands-on look at how legal and political institutions actually function, from case documentation to grassroots organizing. Those experiences showed me how much of the work behind policy and law depends on careful, ethical handling of information about real people. That's the thread I'm pulling on now as a Legal Intern at Osho Law Firm in Charlotte, where I'm continuing to build research and case-preparation skills alongside my coursework.

Outside of legal and political work, I've spent summers coordinating youth programming, from the School of The New York Times' pre-college journalism program to Sotheby's Institute of Art and the Vogue Fashion Institute, roles that sharpened my organization, communication, and leadership skills. On campus, I serve as Event Coordinator for Wealth Education for Black Students and Social Media Chair for the Intersectional Justice Alliance, both of which reflect my commitment to equity and access.

My goal is to go into data privacy and health law. I'm drawn to the questions data science forces us to ask about consent, representation, and who is (and isn't) accounted for in a dataset, and I want to carry that critical lens into law school and beyond. This Data Science Studio portfolio documents that journey: the technical skills I'm building in Python, R, and pandas, and the research questions I'm learning to ask along the way.

When I'm not coding or studying, I love reading, listening to music, playing basketball, and going to concerts.
